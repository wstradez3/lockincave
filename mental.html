Rule of the week

One attempt per zone. If you got stopped at a level, that level is done for the day.

Why it exists: the second and third attempt at the same zone are almost never the plan. They're the urge to be right. Price will give you another level, and another day.

The Cave Confessions

My real mistakes, so you don't have to make them.

Bottom-catching

Six days trying to catch the BTC bottom

I knew the trend was down. I kept buying anyway, because "this has to be the low." Six days of losses. When the bottom finally came, I was too shaken to hold it.

Lesson: no longs at new lows. Wait for the sweep, the reclaim and the close.

Cutting winners

Out at breakeven, then it ran 4R

BTC long from 58,850 with the target at 60,000. Price came back to retest my entry, and I closed it at breakeven out of fear. It then ran all the way to my target.

Lesson: only your stop or your target closes the trade. A retest of your entry is neither.

Rushing

Trying to make money fast

The biggest leak in my trading isn't my analysis. It's rushing: jumping long, jumping short, taking trades in the same zone because I want the money today.

Lesson: one setup a day. You don't need today's trade, you need this month's 20.

Brain hack

Why a loss hurts more than a win feels good

Research on decision-making (loss aversion, Kahneman and Tversky) shows people feel a loss roughly twice as strongly as a win of the same size.

In trading, that's why you cut winners early to "lock it in," and hold losers hoping they come back. Both come from the same place: avoiding the pain of a loss.

The fix: decide stop and target before entry, and let the plan make the decision, not the feeling.
