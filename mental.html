The Mental Room

Your strategy is only as good as the head running it.

I built this room because, honestly, my biggest problems in trading were never the charts. I knew my levels. I knew my bias. And I still found ways to lose: rushing, jumping long then short in the same zone, trying to make the money today.

So this is where I keep myself in check, and where you can too. Before you open a chart, come here. Take a breath. Be honest with yourself for thirty seconds. If that saves you one bad trade a week, it's done its job.

— Waleed

Rule of the week

One attempt per zone. If you got stopped at a level, that level is done for the day.

This one's personal. When I get stopped at a level, everything in me wants to go back in and prove I was right. The second and third attempt are never the plan, they're ego. I've paid for that lesson more times than I'd like to admit. Price will give you another level, and another day.

The Cave Confessions

Real mistakes, from my own account. I share them so you don't have to pay for the same lessons.

Bottom-catching

Six days trying to catch the BTC bottom

I knew the trend was down. I kept buying anyway, every day telling myself "this has to be the low." Six days of losses in a row. And the worst part? When the bottom finally came, I was so shaken I couldn't hold the trade.

Lesson: no longs at new lows. Wait for the sweep, the reclaim and the close.

Cutting winners

Out at breakeven, then it ran 4R

BTC long from 58,850, target at 60,000. Price came back to retest my entry and I panicked, closed it at breakeven. Then I watched it run all the way to my target without me. That one stung.

Lesson: only your stop or your target closes the trade. A retest of your entry is neither.

Rushing

Trying to make money fast

If I'm honest, the biggest leak in my trading isn't my analysis. It's rushing. Jumping long, jumping short, taking trades in the same zone because I want the money now. I'm working on this every single day, and this room is part of that.

Lesson: one setup a day. You don't need today's trade, you need this month's 20.

Brain hack

Why a loss hurts more than a win feels good

There's research on this (loss aversion, Kahneman and Tversky): a loss hits you roughly twice as hard as a win of the same size feels good.

That explains so much. It's why I closed that BTC long at breakeven: I wasn't protecting profit, I was running from the pain of a loss. Same reason people hold losers hoping they come back.

What works for me: stop and target set before I enter, then hands off. The plan decides, not the feeling.
